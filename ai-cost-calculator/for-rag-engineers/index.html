The five cost layers every RAG pipeline hides from you

RAG pipelines have a seductive unit economics problem: each individual cost looks negligible until you multiply it by corpus size or query volume. A RAG system that costs $8 at 1,000 queries per month costs $8,000 at 1,000,000 — and the cost composition shifts dramatically across that range. Engineers who design RAG systems without modeling all five cost layers routinely get surprised in production.

The first layer is initial corpus embedding — the one-time cost of chunking your documents and calling an embedding API or self-hosted model for every chunk. At $0.02 per million tokens (text-embedding-3-small), a 5-million-document corpus averaging 300 tokens per chunk costs about $30 to embed. At $0.13 per million tokens (text-embedding-3-large), the same corpus costs $195. Neither number is a surprise on its own, but most teams discover their corpus is three to five times larger than the working estimate once preprocessing is complete.

The second layer is incremental re-embedding — the ongoing cost of embedding new and updated documents as your knowledge base evolves. For a corpus that turns over 10% of its content monthly, re-embedding at text-embedding-3-small costs a predictable monthly line item that scales with content velocity, not query volume.

The third layer is vector database storage and operations. Managed vector DBs like Pinecone Serverless, Weaviate Cloud, and Qdrant Cloud charge for stored vectors and read operations separately. The storage cost is predictable from corpus size; the read operation cost scales with query volume and the number of top-k candidates you retrieve per query. Fetching top-20 candidates for reranking costs roughly 4× more in read units than fetching top-5 directly — a trade-off that is only visible when you model both options.

The fourth layer is reranker inference. If your pipeline uses a cross-encoder reranker like Cohere Rerank, Jina Reranker, or a self-hosted model to reorder retrieved candidates before passing them to the LLM, every query incurs a reranker call. Cohere Rerank bills at $1 per 1,000 calls; at 500,000 monthly queries that is $500/month — a significant cost center that most RAG architectures never budget for explicitly.

The fifth layer is LLM generation — the most variable cost, driven by context length. Each retrieved chunk you inject into the generation prompt is billable input tokens. Retrieving 5 chunks of 500 tokens each adds 2,500 tokens of context to every query. At GPT-4o rates, that context cost alone is $6.25 per million queries. At GPT-4o Mini it is $0.94 per million queries. Choosing the right generation model — and tuning how many context chunks you pass — is the single highest-leverage cost decision in a mature RAG pipeline.

Five use cases specific to RAG engineers

1. Estimating embedding cost for a new corpus before ingestion

Before running an ingestion pipeline over a large document corpus — legal filings, support tickets, product documentation, internal wikis — you need a cost estimate for the embedding pass. 2. Modeling per-query cost at your target QPS

The per-query cost of a RAG pipeline compounds across four operations: query embedding, vector DB retrieval, optional reranking, and LLM generation. At 10 queries per day the total is essentially zero. At 1 million queries per month, each of those four components contributes meaningfully to a real monthly invoice. Input your query embedding model, average query token length, vector DB provider and top-k, reranker (if any), generation model, and average retrieved context length, and the calculator returns the all-in cost per query and the monthly total at your target volume.

3. Comparing managed vector DB vs. self-hosted on total cost of ownership

Pinecone Serverless, Weaviate Cloud, and Qdrant Cloud all eliminate operational overhead — no cluster management, no embedding index maintenance, no hardware provisioning. That convenience has a price premium over self-hosted. For a 10-million-vector index at 50,000 queries per month, managed options run $30–$60/month depending on provider. A self-hosted Qdrant instance on a $20/month VPS handles the same workload with better latency and zero per-read billing — but adds operational overhead. The calculator surfaces both the dollar cost and the rough ops-hours cost so the build vs. buy decision is quantified on both dimensions.

4. Deciding between small-embedding-plus-reranker vs. large-embedding-alone

A common architecture question in RAG is whether to use a large, high-fidelity embedding model and retrieve a small top-k directly, or use a cheap small embedding model, retrieve a large candidate set, and apply a cross-encoder reranker to get precision. The cost math strongly favors small-plus-reranker at scale: embedding ingestion is 6× cheaper with text-embedding-3-small versus text-embedding-3-large, and the reranker cost at typical query volumes is $5–$50/month — less than the savings on the embedding bill alone. The quality tradeoff is real but often favorable; the calculator makes the cost side concrete so the architecture decision is grounded in numbers.

5. Forecasting RAG vs. fine-tune break-even at scale

Every high-volume RAG pipeline eventually reaches a query volume where fine-tuning the generation model — and collapsing context length — becomes cheaper per month than continuing to inject retrieved context on every call. For a RAG pipeline that injects 3,000 tokens of context per query at GPT-4o pricing, fine-tuning a smaller model and reducing context to 500 tokens saves roughly $6 per thousand queries in generation cost alone. With a fine-tuning investment of $300–$500, the break-even is often 50,000–100,000 queries. The calculator models this transition point with your specific context length, generation model, and query volume so you know when to initiate the fine-tune project.

Frequently asked questions

How do I estimate the token count for my corpus before embedding?

The fastest approximation is 1 token ≈ 4 characters of English text, or roughly 250–300 tokens per standard 200-word paragraph. For PDFs and HTML, extract plain text first — raw file size is a poor predictor of token count because markup and whitespace inflate byte count without adding tokens. Run a sample of 100–200 documents through your chunking pipeline and tokenizer to calibrate a per-document token average, then multiply by your total document count. Budget an additional 10–15% for chunk overlap, which creates duplicate token regions that count toward the embedding bill even though they represent the same content.

Does vector database cost scale with embedding dimension?

Yes — storage cost scales directly with dimension count because each vector requires dimension × 4 bytes (for float32) of storage. A 10-million-vector index at 1,536 dimensions (text-embedding-3-small or Ada-002 output) uses approximately 57 GB of raw vector data, plus overhead for the HNSW index structure (typically 20–40% additional). Switching from 1,536 to 3,072 dimensions (text-embedding-3-large) doubles storage cost. Scalar quantization — supported natively in Qdrant and available in Weaviate — compresses float32 to int8, reducing storage by roughly 4× at a small accuracy tradeoff that is often acceptable for semantic search use cases.

How should I budget for hybrid search (BM25 + dense retrieval)?

Hybrid search adds a keyword-based retrieval pass (BM25 or BM42) alongside dense vector retrieval, then fuses the results. The BM25 pass has no embedding cost but does require an inverted index, which some vector DBs (Weaviate, Qdrant) build automatically while others (Pinecone) handle through a separate sparse vector index. The incremental cost of enabling hybrid search is typically 10–30% additional storage (for the sparse index) and marginal additional latency. For RAG systems where query intent is ambiguous or domain terminology is distinctive, hybrid search materially improves recall with a small cost premium — budget it as a fixed per-month addition rather than a per-query surcharge.

Is the calculator accurate for self-hosted embedding models?

For self-hosted models running on your own GPU (sentence-transformers, BGE, E5, or quantized versions of larger models), the cost is GPU-hour × embedding throughput, not a per-token API charge. A single A10G GPU (roughly $0.75/hr on Lambda Labs or Vast.ai) can embed approximately 500,000–2,000,000 tokens per hour depending on the model and batch size. The calculator supports a custom GPU-hour cost mode where you enter your GPU cost and throughput benchmark to derive a per-token cost equivalent that drops directly into the comparison table alongside managed API options.

What is the cheapest production-grade RAG stack for a bootstrapped team?

For a team that wants production reliability without a large infrastructure budget: text-embedding-3-small for corpus embedding, self-hosted Qdrant on a $20–$30/month VPS, no managed reranker (use a lightweight cross-encoder via HuggingFace Inference Endpoints instead, roughly $0.02/call), and GPT-4o Mini or Llama 3.3 70B on Together AI for generation. At 50,000 queries per month, this stack runs approximately $40–$70/month all-in — compared to $150–$250/month for the fully managed equivalent. The trade-off is operational effort: Qdrant upgrades, vector index backups, and VPS monitoring are on your team rather than the provider's SLA.
