Why an AI prompt enhancer matters in 2026

Every major image model — Midjourney v7, DALL-E 3, Flux 1.1 Pro, Stable Diffusion XL, Sora — quietly rewrites your prompt before rendering. They each have a "prompt expander" baked in, and it works differently in each one. Midjourney rewards keyword-stacked tags ("cinematic, golden hour, 35mm, shallow depth of field"); DALL-E rewards natural-language sentences; Flux rewards dense paragraph descriptions and ignores negative prompts; Stable Diffusion XL still leans on the old "subject, style, modifiers, ((emphasis))" pattern and uses a separate negative prompt. A prompt that's great in Midjourney often gives mediocre output in DALL-E and vice versa. An AI prompt enhancer's job is to take your one-line idea and produce the version of it that the specific model you're targeting will respond to best.

What separates a good Midjourney prompt from a bad one

A good Midjourney v7 prompt is dense but coherent: subject (clearly named), action, environment, art-style or photography style, lighting, lens / camera, mood, --ar X:Y --style raw. The classic mistake is stacking 40 unrelated modifiers — "cinematic, anime, hyperrealistic, watercolor, low-poly" — which forces the model to average several incompatible looks. The enhancer's style preset picks one coherent visual language at a time, then layers the supporting modifiers that go with it. For SDXL it also generates a sensible negative prompt (deformed hands, watermark, low contrast, blurry, jpeg artifacts) tuned to the style. For DALL-E and Flux, no negative prompt is emitted — those models don't use them.

What about prompts for video models — Sora, Veo, Runway?

Video prompts work differently from image prompts. The model needs a shot description (wide / medium / close-up), camera movement (dolly, crane, handheld), subject motion, environment, lighting, and an explicit duration cue. The Sora / Veo 3 setting in this tool produces that shot-list-style prompt: opening shot, motion, camera direction, sound design hint, and a final beat. It's the same source idea but compiled into the temporal grammar video models understand.
