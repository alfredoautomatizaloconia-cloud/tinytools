5 Disclosure Scenarios Every AI Agency Faces

Generic compliance guides are written for enterprise legal teams, not agencies shipping deliverables daily. Here are the five situations where AI agency owners specifically need compliant disclosure labels.

📋

Client Deliverable Packages (Copy, Images, Video)

When your agency hands off a content batch — blog posts, ad creatives, social media assets, product descriptions — to a client who will publish them to EU audiences, EU AI Act Article 50 creates an obligation along that entire chain. Your client is the publisher, but you're the one who generated the content. Including ready-made disclosure labels with each deliverable protects your clients from compliance exposure and positions your agency as a responsible AI partner — a genuine competitive differentiator as the August 2026 deadline approaches.

🏷️

White-Label AI Services and Reseller Agreements

Many AI agencies resell AI-generated content under a client's brand, with no visible mention of AI. That's still AI-generated content under the EU AI Act — the white-label arrangement doesn't eliminate the disclosure requirement; it just shifts questions about who is responsible. Building disclosure language into your white-label SOWs and deliverable handoffs ensures the downstream publisher (your client) has what they need to comply, and that your contract clearly assigns that responsibility. Client-Facing Case Studies and Portfolio Samples

Your agency website's portfolio is almost certainly full of AI-generated content presented as examples of your work. If those case studies include AI-generated text excerpts, AI-created images, or AI-produced video demos, every one of them is subject to Article 50 when viewed by EU visitors — and your site almost certainly has EU visitors. 🎙️

AI Voice-Overs and Video Production for Clients

AI-generated voice and video content face the strictest disclosure requirements under the EU AI Act — these are treated as synthetic media with high potential for deception. If your agency produces explainer videos, podcast intros, YouTube ads, or social media video clips using AI voice or AI-generated visuals, every piece needs a disclosure both in the content metadata and visibly to the viewer. The generator handles AI Voice and AI Video as first-class content types, outputting disclosure labels that satisfy both the "visible to the user" and "machine-readable" requirements of Article 50.

📢

Agency's Own AI-Generated Marketing and Thought Leadership

Your own blog posts, LinkedIn articles, newsletters, and X threads showcasing AI capabilities or agency insights — if they're written or significantly assisted by AI — need disclosure too. Many agency owners skip this because it feels awkward to label your own content. But the EU AI Act doesn't carve out an exemption for companies that build AI products; it applies to all AI-generated content reaching EU readers. Getting your own house in order is also the right way to demonstrate the transparency your clients should expect from you.

Common Questions from AI Agency Owners

My agency is US-based. Does the EU AI Act actually apply to us?

Yes, if content you produce reaches EU users — which it almost certainly does if your clients publish to general audiences online. The EU AI Act has extraterritorial reach: it applies based on where the output is consumed, not where the agency is incorporated. If your client publishes AI-generated content to a website accessible in the EU, the disclosure obligation follows that content. The practical advice for most US agencies: assume Article 50 applies to any content that goes live on the web.

My client contract says they're responsible for compliance. Does that protect me?

Contract clauses allocating compliance responsibility between parties are valid and enforceable between you and your client, but they don't override your obligations to third parties or regulators under EU law. If your agency is the one generating the AI content, you may still face questions about your role in the supply chain. More practically: if your client gets fined and the investigation traces back to your deliverables, your contract language becomes a legal dispute rather than protection. Providing disclosure labels as part of your standard deliverable package is far cheaper than litigation.

For web-delivered content, use the HTML badge and JSON-LD schema instead. The generator produces all three formats in the same interface — pick what fits the delivery medium.

We use Midjourney for client images. Does image disclosure work differently?

Under EU AI Act Article 50, AI-generated images require disclosure when there's a risk the viewer might believe the image is real or human-created — which applies to almost all photorealistic AI images. The disclosure can be a visible watermark, a caption, or an image metadata tag alongside a machine-readable JSON-LD schema on the page. The generator handles AI Image as a specific content type with output formatted for web use. For images embedded in documents or PDFs, use the plain-text caption format.

The plain-text output is designed to be portable — you can include it in an SOW addendum, a deliverable handoff checklist, or an agency MSA as boilerplate disclosure language. We always recommend having a qualified attorney review any language that goes into a binding contract, but as a starting point, the plain-text format is written to be clear, specific, and legally descriptive rather than technical jargon.
