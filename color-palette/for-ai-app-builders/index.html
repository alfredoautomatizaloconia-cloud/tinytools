Why Your AI-Built App Looks Like Everyone Else's

Lovable, Bolt, and Cursor generate functional UIs in minutes. The problem: they all pull from the same Tailwind default tokens. Indigo-600 buttons. Slate-900 backgrounds. Gray-400 muted text. After a hundred AI-built apps launched in the same week, users can smell the template from a mile away.

A custom color palette is the fastest way to make your app feel intentional — not generated. And for AI app builders who move at 100 mph, spending 45 minutes in Figma picking accessible color combinations is time that doesn't exist.

Pain #1
Your Lovable or Bolt app uses the default Tailwind indigo/slate theme. Every investor, user, and competitor can immediately tell it's an AI-generated template with no brand identity.
Solution

Pain #2
You're not a designer. Every time you try to pick a color palette by eye, something looks off — either the contrast is too low for accessibility or the accent color clashes with the background in dark mode.
Solution
TinyTools automatically calculates WCAG AA contrast ratios for every foreground/background combination it outputs. No more failing the accessibility check at 2 AM before a ProductHunt launch. Every palette it generates is ready for production out of the box.
Pain #3
You're prompting Lovable or Cursor to "make it look more professional" but the AI keeps generating arbitrary hex values that break your brand across components — buttons are one blue, links are another.
Solution
Generate a structured palette once, then paste the CSS variable definitions at the top of your Lovable/Cursor prompt: "Use these CSS variables for all colors: --primary: #6d28d9, --primary-hover: #5b21b6…" The AI follows the system, not its own defaults. Every component stays on-brand.
Pain #4
Your app needs both light and dark mode, and manually computing the dark-mode equivalents of your light-mode palette (adjusting lightness, saturation, and opacity layers) takes hours in HSL math.
Solution
TinyTools generates paired light and dark variants for every color in the palette. You get a single CSS variable block with [data-theme="dark"] overrides ready to drop into your Tailwind config — no manual HSL arithmetic needed.
Pain #5
You're using shadcn/ui components in your Cursor or Bolt app. The shadcn CSS token names don't match standard Tailwind names, and manually mapping your palette to --background, --foreground, --card, --primary etc. is tedious.
Solution
TinyTools exports your palette in shadcn/ui token format directly — just paste the output into your project's globals.css to override the shadcn defaults. All your Button, Card, Input, and Badge components instantly adopt your brand colors.
