Title Tag Length and SEO Best Practices for 2026 — A Field Guide

May 5, 2026 · 9 min read · By TinyTools

Every SEO blog post on the first page of Google still tells you the same thing: “Keep your title tag under 60 characters.” That number was approximately right in 2014. In 2026 it is, at best, a folk theorem — and at worst it is silently costing you clicks, because Google does not count characters and never has. It counts pixels. And the pixel budget shifts every time Google adjusts its SERP, which now happens roughly twice a year.

This is the field guide to what actually matters for SEO title tags in 2026: how Google really truncates them, what mobile and AI Overviews do differently, why your titles get rewritten about 60% of the time, and the seven patterns that survive every kind of SERP cut.

The 60-character rule is folk wisdom — here are the real numbers

Google’s desktop SERP container for the title link is ~600 px wide. Mobile is ~480 px wide. The font is Arial-equivalent at 20 px desktop / 18 px mobile. None of those values map cleanly to a character count, because Latin letters are not equally wide:

  • i, l, I, 1 are about 4–5 px each
  • e, a, n, o are about 10 px
  • W, M, capital letters in general, are about 16–18 px
  • Spaces are 5 px

That asymmetry is why “ALL CAPS HEADLINES GET CUT EARLIER” is not a stylistic suggestion — it is a literal pixel fact. A 60-character title in all caps overruns the desktop SERP by 30–40 px and gets truncated mid-word. A 65-character title in mostly-lowercase fits comfortably.

The takeaway: the front of your title is the only part that is guaranteed to render anywhere. Everything past character ~35 is a bonus.

The 60% rewrite tax

The other change since the 60-character era is that Google rewrites titles in the SERP about 60% of the time. It started in August 2021, plateaued briefly, then crept upward as the AI search rollout made “the title we crawled” and “the title the user sees” diverge further.

What Google substitutes in:

  1. Your H1 — most common, especially when the title and H1 disagree
  2. Anchor text from internal links — second most common
  3. The first <strong> phrase in the first paragraph
  4. OpenGraph og:title if the regular title looks templated
  5. A model-generated rewrite combining the query and your H1 (new in 2024, expanded in late 2025)

You cannot opt out of rewriting. You can only make rewriting less likely by removing the things that trigger it: ALL CAPS, brand-stuffing (Best Widget | TopWidgets | Widget Co | 2026), boilerplate suffixes that repeat across thousands of pages, and titles that disagree with the H1 by more than a few words.

The mobile-first front-loading rule

Mobile is now ~64% of Google traffic. On a 480 px container the visible portion of your title is roughly the first 35 characters before truncation kicks in. Anything past the ellipsis still gets indexed and still counts for ranking — but it does not earn the click. Front-load.

Things to stop doing in 2026

  • The brand-name pipe-spam suffix. ... | YourBrand | YourBrand HQ | YourBrand 2026 reads as low quality to both Google and to a human. Pick one suffix, separated by a single em-dash.
  • Date stuffing in the slug AND title AND H1. Once is a freshness signal; three times is templated boilerplate. Google’s 2024 helpful-content classifier reads the latter as low effort.
  • Optimizing every title to exactly 60 characters. A site where every title is 58–60 characters is a textbook fingerprint of programmatic SEO content. Vary length 35–65 across your site; shorter titles often win on mobile.
  • Putting the year first. 2026: Best Practices for ... looks like a list-bait fingerprint. Year goes near the end.
  • Pipe-only separators. A decade of SEO tooling pumped out so many | Brand titles that Google’s rewrite model now disproportionately replaces them. Em-dash separator, single brand suffix.

One more thing: the AI search citation question

The reason title tags are more important in 2026, not less, is that AI search assistants — Perplexity, ChatGPT search, Google AI Overviews — all surface citations as small chips that show only the title. There is no meta description. There is no snippet. Just the title.

If your title doesn’t match the user’s question closely enough, the model will summarize your content without citing you. The page still ranks. You just don’t get credit for it. The fix: write the title for the question the AI is most likely to answer with your page.

TL;DR

  • Pixel width, not 60 characters: budget ~600 px desktop, ~480 px mobile, ~340 px on AI Overview chips.
  • Front-load the keyword in the first 35 characters. Mobile cuts there.
  • Google rewrites ~60% of titles. Reduce the rewrite tax by removing brand-spam, ALL CAPS, and H1/title disagreement.
  • Em-dash separator beats pipes in 2026. One brand suffix only.
  • Number + Noun + Outcome is the most rewrite-resistant pattern.
  • AI search citations show only the title — match it to the user’s question.
