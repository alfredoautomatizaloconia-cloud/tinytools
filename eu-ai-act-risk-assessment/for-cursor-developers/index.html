The Cursor Developer's EU AI Act Problem

Cursor has changed how fast AI features get shipped. A developer can build a document analyzer, a candidate screening tool, or a credit scoring assistant in a single focused session. The speed is the point — but it also means compliance rarely enters the picture until much later.

The EU AI Act doesn't care how you built your product. It cares what your product does and who it affects. If your Cursor-built app helps make decisions about people in the EU — in employment, credit, healthcare, education, or legal contexts — it may already be a regulated AI system. The Annex III high-risk categories are specific and surprisingly easy to fall into, especially for solo developers and small teams building fast.

The good news: most Cursor-built apps will land in the minimal or limited risk tier, which means few to no mandatory compliance obligations. The bad news: you won't know which tier you're in until you actually check. The assessment below takes two minutes and tells you exactly where you stand — with a plain-language breakdown you can show to investors, clients, or EU users.

⚠ High Risk

Mandatory Compliance

Conformity assessments, human oversight requirements, documentation obligations, registration in the EU database. Applies to Annex III use cases.

⚡ Limited Risk

Transparency Required

You must tell EU users they're interacting with an AI system. Applies to chatbots, AI-generated content, and emotion recognition tools.

✓ Minimal Risk

No Mandatory Obligations

Most AI apps fall here. Voluntary codes of conduct apply; no mandatory registration or conformity assessment required. Keep shipping.

5 EU AI Act Scenarios Specific to Cursor Developers

Use Case 1

AI Resume Screener or Candidate Ranking Tool — Built Over a Weekend, Now Used by Clients

Cursor makes it trivially fast to build a tool that ingests CVs, runs them through an LLM, and returns a ranked shortlist. If clients use this tool to inform actual hiring decisions — even with human review of the output — you've likely built a high-risk AI system under Annex III, Category 4 (Employment and Workers Management). The trigger isn't whether a human makes the final call; it's whether the AI output materially influences a decision about a natural person's employment. This is one of the easiest Cursor-built apps to accidentally land in the high-risk tier. Run the assessment immediately if you've shipped anything in this space.

Use Case 2

AI SaaS Product with EU Users — Shipped Fast, Compliance Never Considered

You built an AI-powered productivity tool, writing assistant, or business intelligence platform using Cursor. It has paying users in Germany, France, or the Netherlands. For most general-purpose productivity apps, you're in the minimal risk tier — no mandatory obligations, keep growing. But if your product's core value proposition involves analyzing data about individuals to generate recommendations (credit risk, insurance pricing, behavioral predictions), your risk tier is determined by the output's domain and purpose, not the technology stack. The assessment maps your app's function to the EU Act's category system so you know exactly where you stand before you hit scale.

Use Case 3

AI Customer Service Bot for a Client — Deployed on Their Site, Talking to Their EU Customers

Cursor developers frequently build AI chatbots for clients: live chat widgets, support ticket triage systems, and FAQ bots that use RAG over the client's documentation. As the developer who built and possibly deployed this system, you may carry obligations as an AI provider or deployer depending on your contractual relationship with the client. The limited risk tier's Article 50 transparency obligation almost certainly applies: EU users interacting with your bot must be told they're talking to an AI system. This is straightforward to comply with, but "straightforward" assumes you know about it. The assessment identifies your role and your specific obligations in the deployer chain.

Use Case 4

AI Document Analyzer for Legal or Financial Use Cases — Contracts, Invoices, Credit Agreements

AI-assisted document analysis is one of the most common Cursor-built tools: contract review assistants, invoice classifiers, legal clause extractors, and financial document summarizers. The Act's risk classification depends on how the output is used. A tool that extracts and organizes contract metadata for search is almost certainly minimal risk. A tool that produces a compliance recommendation or creditworthiness assessment that a human then acts on may be limited or high risk depending on the domain (credit decisions fall under Annex III, Category 5: Access to Essential Services). Build these tools, but understand the specific compliance posture before deploying to EU financial or legal clients.

Use Case 5

AI Code Review or Developer Productivity Tool — Teams Using It to Make Engineering Decisions

Many Cursor developers naturally build meta-tools: AI-powered PR reviewers, code quality dashboards, commit summarizers, and sprint planning assistants that assign tasks to developers. A code review tool that flags bugs is minimal risk. A tool that scores developer performance, ranks team members by productivity metrics, or recommends promotion or performance management actions is squarely in the employment/worker management category (Annex III, Category 4). The performance assessment use case is where developer productivity tools cross into high-risk territory. Know where your tool lands before selling it to HR or engineering leadership at EU-based companies.

FAQ: EU AI Act for Cursor Developers

I use Cursor to write code, but the AI features in my product come from the OpenAI or Anthropic API. Am I an AI provider or a deployer?

You're a deployer — you're using an existing AI model (the provider's product) and deploying it within your specific application context. Cursor is your development tool; the model API is the AI component; your app is the deployed AI system. As a deployer, your obligations depend on the risk tier of your specific use case, not the model's general capabilities.

My app is in beta and only has a few EU users. Do I still need to comply?

The Act applies to systems "placed on the market or put into service" in the EU. A public beta accessible to EU users is generally considered being put into service. Size and user count don't create exemptions — risk category does. If your beta is minimal risk, you have no mandatory obligations regardless of user count. If it's high-risk, you need to comply from the moment EU users can access it.

I built an AI feature for a client in the EU. They're deploying it — am I still responsible?

It depends on the contractual structure. If you built and handed off the system as a one-time development project, the client deploying it becomes the deployer. If you're maintaining, hosting, or operating the AI component as a service, you may share deployer obligations. The assessment includes a "who is the deployer" flow that covers contractor and agency relationships.

What are the fines for non-compliance, and do they actually apply to small developers?

For violations related to prohibited AI practices: up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover. For high-risk system violations: up to €15 million or 3% of turnover. National market surveillance authorities can investigate any deployer, regardless of company size. However, enforcement in practice will likely target higher-profile cases first. Being proactively compliant is far cheaper than being a test case for enforcement.

Cursor itself uses AI under the hood. Does that affect my compliance status as a developer using Cursor?

No — your use of Cursor to write code doesn't create EU AI Act obligations for you. The Act is about AI systems you deploy to end users, not AI tools you use internally in your development workflow. Your Cursor subscription is a developer productivity tool; the AI system regulated by the Act is the product you're building with it.
