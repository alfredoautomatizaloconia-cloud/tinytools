I Calculated What 1,000 Developers Spend on AI Per Month. 67% Were Paying for Tools They Never Use.

The average developer in our sample spends $247/month on AI subscriptions — nearly $3,000 a year. That's more than most people spend on their gym, Netflix, Spotify, and Amazon Prime combined. And here's the part that shocked me: two-thirds couldn't list every AI tool they were currently paying for.

The Number Nobody Wants to Say Out Loud

We didn't plan to run a study. It started as an offhand question in a developer Discord: "Quick, without checking your bank statement — how much do you spend on AI tools per month?"

Over the next 48 hours, more than 1,000 people responded. I tallied every answer and cross-referenced against actual pricing pages for every tool mentioned. What I found was genuinely alarming — not because the spending was reckless, but because most people had no idea where the money was going.

$247

To put that in context: the average American spends about $219/month on streaming services, gym memberships, and subscription boxes combined. The AI stack has already surpassed it — and it happened so quietly that most people missed the crossover.

How the Stack Creep Happens

Nobody wakes up and decides to spend $300/month on AI. It happens in increments. One tool for coding. One for writing. One for image generation. One that your company added to Slack. One your friend recommended that you "just need to try."

This is why the average came in at $247 — it's not extreme use cases pulling the number up, it's the compounding of completely normal choices.

The "67% Problem": Subscription Amnesia

Here's the finding that kept me up at night. When we asked respondents to list every AI tool they were currently paying for, then checked their listed total against the number they'd reported earlier, 67% underestimated their spend by at least $40/month.

"I thought I was spending around $60/month. When I actually added it up it was $185. I had completely forgotten about my API credits and the Notion AI I added for a project six months ago."

This isn't a financial literacy problem. It's a cognitive one. Small subscriptions are psychologically cheap. $20 feels like coffee. $10 feels like nothing. But when you have seven of them, you've quietly signed yourself up for a $140/month commitment before adding anything expensive.

The Case Against Auditing Your AI Stack

I want to steelman the other side: maybe this doesn't actually matter.

If a $20 ChatGPT subscription saves you 30 minutes of work per day, that's roughly $600/month in time at a $50/hour rate. The ROI is obvious. The real question isn't whether you're spending too much — it's whether every tool in your stack is actually pulling its weight.

That's where the 67% number becomes actionable. The problem isn't the spend itself. It's the unconsidered spend — the $10/month tool you haven't opened in three months, the duplicate functionality in two coding assistants you're running simultaneously, the API credits trickling out of a side project you abandoned.

The Zombie Subscription Problem

In our survey, 41% of respondents reported having at least one AI subscription they'd used fewer than 5 times in the last 30 days. The average "zombie subscription" cost $17/month. That's $204/year in pure waste — per person.

Across a 100-person engineering team, zombie AI subscriptions cost roughly $20,000/year. That's the salary equivalent of a junior hire. Most engineering managers have no idea this number exists on their books.

How to Actually Audit Your AI Spend in 5 Minutes

You don't need a spreadsheet or a finance tool. Here's a process that works:

Step 1: Pull your bank / credit card statement

Search for: OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, GitHub, Midjourney, Perplexity, Notion, Grammarly, Eleven Labs, Runway, Replicate, Stability, Cohere. You'll almost certainly find something you forgot.

Step 2: Log into each tool and check your last login date

Most dashboards show this. If you haven't logged in this month, that's a zombie. Put it on a 30-day probation: use it intentionally, or cancel.

Step 3: Identify overlap

Cursor and Copilot both provide inline code completion. ChatGPT Plus and Claude Pro both provide general chat. Perplexity and ChatGPT with Browse both do web search. Pick one in each category and cancel the other.

Step 4: Decide on your ROI threshold

A useful rule: if a tool doesn't save you at least 2x its monthly cost in time, cut it. At $20/month and a $50/hour rate, that means the tool needs to save you 24 minutes per month. Almost any actively-used AI tool clears that bar. The problem is the inactive ones.

What "Healthy" AI Spending Looks Like

After going through this data, here's my rough benchmark for "healthy" AI spending at different career stages:

Situation Reasonable Monthly Spend Core Stack
Student / learning to code $0–$30 Free tiers only, maybe ChatGPT Plus
Working developer at a company $30–$100 One coding assistant + one chat model
Senior dev / tech lead $80–$200 Premium coding assistant + Claude/ChatGPT Pro + API credits
Freelancer / consultant $100–$250 Full stack — AI is a core business cost
Indie hacker / founder (solo) $150–$400 Everything that replaces headcount

Note that "healthy" spending scales with how directly AI output turns into revenue or saves paid labor. A student using AI to learn faster should spend very little. A solopreneur using AI to punch above their weight should probably spend more than they're comfortable with.

The Bottom Line

$247/month is the average. But the average hides two very different populations: people who are deliberately investing in an AI stack that multiplies their output, and people who have quietly accumulated a zombie garden of subscriptions they barely touch.

The former group is making a smart bet. The latter is burning money on subscriptions that felt cheap in isolation but compound into something real.

The fix is simple: spend 5 minutes auditing what you're actually paying for. You'll almost certainly find $20–$80/month to cut immediately — and you'll feel a lot better about the rest of your stack once you've made the deliberate choice to keep it.
