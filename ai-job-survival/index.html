How is the AI risk score calculated?

    
The score is derived from a deterministic rule engine that evaluates your job title against 6 risk dimensions used in academic automation research (Frey & Osborne, McKinsey Global Institute, and World Economic Forum reports):

    
High-risk factors: routine cognitive tasks, data processing, pattern matching, content generation, rule-following, predictable physical labor.

    
Low-risk factors: physical dexterity in unpredictable environments, emotional intelligence, creative judgment, complex stakeholder management, novel problem solving.

    
Same job title = same score every time. No AI, no randomness, fully reproducible and shareable.


    
Which jobs are safest from AI?

    
Jobs requiring physical presence in variable environments (electrician, plumber, surgeon), deep human trust (therapist, social worker, clergy), and highly creative original thinking (novelist, conceptual artist, research scientist) score lowest on replacement probability. These combine physical unpredictability with human judgment in ways that remain extremely difficult to automate.


    
Which jobs are most at risk?

    
Jobs centered on processing, analyzing, or generating text, data, or images at scale are highest risk: data entry clerks, paralegal researchers, copywriters producing commodity content, basic customer support agents, and entry-level code generation roles. These tasks map cleanly onto current LLM and automation capabilities.


    
Does this mean I'll lose my job?

    
Not necessarily. Historical automation waves (agricultural, industrial, digital) eliminated categories of tasks but created new categories of work. The most likely outcome is your job description changes significantly over 5–10 years rather than disappearing entirely. The question isn't "will AI replace me" — it's "what parts of my job should I augment with AI before someone else does?"
