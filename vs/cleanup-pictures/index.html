TinyTools vs Cleanup.pictures (2026)

Updated June 1, 2026 · ~6 min read · Honest comparison, not a takedown
The 30-second answer. Cleanup.pictures is a focused AI inpainting tool — you brush over an unwanted object in a photo and its generative model fills in the gap with convincing background. It does that one thing extremely well, handles complex scenes, and offers a clean free tier for casual use. If you regularly need to remove people, logos, watermarks, power lines, or stray objects from photos and want results that hold up in professional contexts, Cleanup.pictures earns its Pro price. TinyTools' Background Remover does a different job — it isolates the main subject from the background, not mid-scene objects — but it's free, requires no account, and handles the most common image-editing request in a browser tab. For AI text output around images (detection, description, metadata), TinyTools' AI Text Detector and AI Spend Calculator are relevant if you're evaluating whether a paid AI image tool subscription is worth the cost. The tools are genuinely complementary: Cleanup.pictures for precision object removal, TinyTools for the surrounding workflow that's free.

What each one actually is

Cleanup.pictures is a browser-based AI inpainting tool built on generative diffusion models. The core workflow is simple: upload a photo, paint a brush over the region you want removed (an ex in a group shot, a tourist in a landscape, a logo on a product, a power line across a sky), and the AI synthesizes plausible replacement content from the surrounding pixels. The result is often indistinguishable from a photo where the object was never present — provided the background is not too complex and the removed region is not too large relative to the image. The tool was among the early consumer-accessible diffusion inpainting tools and built a substantial user base before the market filled with competitors. The free tier allows processing of images up to a limited resolution and applies a light watermark or resolution cap on downloads; the Pro plan removes these limits and enables batch processing. Cleanup.pictures works entirely in the browser — no app to download, no Photoshop required — and targets photographers, marketers, social media creators, and e-commerce teams who need fast, convincing object removal without hiring a retoucher. Competitors in the same niche include Adobe Firefly's generative fill (better integration if you're already in the Adobe ecosystem), Microsoft Designer's erase tool, and various Photoshop Content-Aware Fill workflows for users who already own the software.
TinyTools is a collection of free, no-signup browser utilities. The most relevant tools for someone evaluating Cleanup.pictures are: Background Remover (removes the entire background behind a subject — different from object removal, but the most common free alternative people are searching for when they land on AI image tools), AI Image Upscaler (upscales images to higher resolution using AI — useful if your source photo is too low-res to retouch convincingly regardless of which tool you use), and AI Spend Calculator (if you're already paying for Cleanup Pro, Canva Pro, Adobe, and Midjourney, this shows your full monthly AI image tool cost and helps you decide what to cut or consolidate). TinyTools does not do mid-scene object removal or inpainting. These are genuinely different capabilities. If you need to paint over a stranger in a landscape photo and have the AI regenerate the sky and grass behind them, TinyTools does not currently do that. Background Remover handles the subject-from-background case (product photos, profile pictures, cut-outs); Cleanup.pictures handles the object-in-scene case (removing things from within the photo).

Pricing comparison

Cleanup.pictures offers a free tier with no account required, which is genuinely useful for occasional, lower-resolution work. The primary constraint on the free tier is output resolution — processed images are capped at a smaller dimension, which can produce visibly soft results when viewed at full size or printed. The Pro plan at approximately $9/month lifts the resolution cap, enables batch processing, and provides API access for teams integrating object removal into automated workflows. By AI tool subscription standards, $9/month is on the affordable end of the category, especially given the quality of output and the breadth of tasks it handles.

When Cleanup.pictures is the better choice

  • You need to remove an object that's inside the photo, not the background itself. This is the clearest distinction. If you want to erase a person standing in a crowd, remove a traffic cone from a street scene, clean a power line from a landscape, or delete a logo from a product shot, Cleanup.pictures does exactly this. No current TinyTools utility handles mid-scene inpainting. If this is your task, Cleanup.pictures (or a comparable AI inpainting tool) is the right tool — full stop.
  • You need high-resolution output for print or commercial use. Cleanup.pictures Pro delivers full-resolution processed images suitable for print, large-format display, or commercial photography. TinyTools' Background Remover also outputs full resolution, but it does a fundamentally different task. When the job is object removal at pro quality, Cleanup.pictures' Pro tier is the appropriate tool.
  • You're processing photos at volume — e-commerce, real estate, event photography. Batch processing on the Pro plan lets you queue multiple images rather than uploading and brushing each one individually. Combined with the API, this makes Cleanup.pictures viable as a component in an automated image pipeline for teams producing high volumes of retouched images (product catalogs, listing photos, event galleries).
  • You want to remove watermarks or text overlaid on images. Cleanup.pictures is frequently used to remove watermarks, timestamps, subtitles burned into video frames, and other text overlaid on images. The AI fill works well when the region underneath the text can be plausibly reconstructed from surrounding pixels. This is a distinct use case that doesn't overlap with background removal.
  • You need a mobile-optimized image editing experience. Cleanup.pictures' mobile browser experience is well-optimized — the brush tool works reliably on touch screens and the upload/download flow is smooth on iOS and Android. If you're editing images on a phone or tablet, Cleanup.pictures is a more ergonomic choice than most desktop-oriented tools.
  • You value a focused, single-purpose tool over a utility collection. Cleanup.pictures does one thing and has spent significant engineering effort doing it well. The inpainting quality — especially on complex backgrounds like grass, foliage, and textured surfaces — is genuinely impressive and noticeably better than running a general image editor's Content-Aware Fill on difficult regions. If output quality on hard inpainting tasks matters, the specialization pays off.

When TinyTools is the better choice

  • Your actual need is background removal, not object removal. The most common image editing request — isolating a product, person, or subject on a transparent or white background — is background removal, not object removal. These are different operations. TinyTools' Background Remover handles this for free, with no account, at full resolution, and with no watermark on the output. If you searched for "Cleanup.pictures alternative" because you need to remove a background from a product photo or profile picture, TinyTools may be what you actually want.
  • Your source image is too low-resolution to retouch effectively. Object removal is only useful if the resulting image is sharp enough to use. If your photo is 600×400 pixels, even perfect inpainting will produce a blurry result at print size. The AI Image Upscaler can help you bring a low-res image up to a workable resolution before you decide whether it's worth retouching at all. Run this step first — it costs nothing and can save you the frustration of retouching an image that was always going to be too soft.

For anyone working with client photos, personal images, or internal company assets under data handling policies, this matters. Cleanup.pictures uploads images to its servers for processing; TinyTools does not.
  • You want to audit your AI tool spend before adding another subscription. If you're weighing whether Cleanup.pictures Pro at ~$9/month is worth it alongside what you're already paying for Canva, Adobe, or other AI image tools, the AI Spend Calculator gives you the full monthly number to evaluate against. Three concrete use cases

1. A photographer removing a stranger from a landscape shot

You've got a great landscape photo but a tourist walked into the frame. The person occupies about 10% of the image and is standing in front of a grass field and distant mountains — a complex background the AI needs to reconstruct. You want the finished image to be indistinguishable from one where the person was never there. Recommendation: Cleanup.pictures. This is exactly the task its AI is optimized for, and it handles complex natural backgrounds well. Brush over the person, let the model run, and check the result — on most outdoor scenes with natural backgrounds, the fill is convincing enough that no further editing is needed. If the source image is smaller than 12MP and you want to print it, run it through AI Image Upscaler first to give the inpainting model more pixels to work with.

2. An e-commerce seller needing product photos on white backgrounds

You've photographed 20 products at home against a textured tablecloth and need clean white-background images for your Shopify store. The task is background removal, not object removal. Recommendation: TinyTools' Background Remover. This is a background removal job, not an inpainting job — Cleanup.pictures isn't the right tool here. Background Remover handles this free, processes images client-side (no uploads), and outputs transparent PNGs you can place on any background color you need. If your product photos are low-resolution from a phone camera, run them through AI Image Upscaler first to get sharper edges on the cutout.

3. A social media manager removing a watermarked stock photo overlay

You found the ideal image for a post, but the free version has a watermark burned over the center. You want to remove it cleanly. Recommendation: Cleanup.pictures — carefully, and only if you have the license to use the underlying image. Cleanup.pictures handles text-and-logo overlays reasonably well when the background underneath can be plausibly reconstructed. However, removing a watermark from a stock photo you don't have a license for raises copyright and terms-of-service issues regardless of which tool you use. If you hold the license and the watermark is a processing artifact from a preview, Cleanup.pictures is the practical tool for removal. If you don't hold the license, the right answer is to purchase it or find a royalty-free alternative — not to use an AI tool to strip the protection.
