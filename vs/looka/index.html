TinyTools vs Looka (2026)

Updated May 23, 2026 · ~6 min read · Honest comparison, not a takedown
The 30-second answer. Looka is a paid AI-powered brand identity platform — you describe your business, it generates logo concepts plus a full brand kit (social media assets, business cards, email signatures), sold as a subscription or one-time package. TinyTools doesn't generate logos. What it does offer is the free supporting layer: a color palette generator for locking in brand colors, a favicon generator for converting any logo PNG into a complete icon set, and a background remover for cleaning up logo files. If you need a logo and brand assets created from scratch with no design skills required, Looka is a strong option. If you already have a logo and need the web-deployment assets, or you're not ready to spend money yet, TinyTools covers what you actually need for free.

What each one actually is

Looka (looka.com) is an end-to-end AI brand identity platform built for founders, freelancers, and small businesses who want a professional visual identity without hiring a designer. You input your company name, industry, preferred colors, and style preferences, and Looka's AI generates a set of logo options — icon-plus-wordmark combinations, monograms, and abstract marks — each paired with a color palette and typeface. You refine your chosen concept in a browser editor, then purchase a download tier. Looka's main differentiator from simpler logo tools is its Brand Kit: a subscription add-on (~$96/year) that unlocks an ongoing library of branded templates for social media posts, LinkedIn banners, email signatures, letterhead, and business cards, all pre-populated with your logo, colors, and fonts. Looka also integrates with print-on-demand services for physical merchandise. All downloads require a paid plan — the preview experience is watermarked.
TinyTools is a collection of free, no-signup browser utilities covering individual steps in the web and brand workflow. The three most directly relevant here: the color palette generator produces a harmonious palette from any seed hex or image URL, outputting hex codes ready to paste into Figma or CSS. The favicon generator takes any PNG or SVG and exports the complete icon size set (16×16 through 512×512) plus a web manifest snippet for copy-paste into <head>. The background remover strips the background from any image entirely in-browser, with no file upload to an external server. TinyTools doesn't generate logos — it processes and deploys the logo you already have.

When Looka is the better choice

Looka genuinely excels in a set of scenarios that TinyTools doesn't touch. If any of the following describe your situation, Looka is the right tool.

  • You need a logo and have no design skills or existing assets. This is the primary use case and Looka handles it well. The AI generates dozens of logo concepts from your brief, the customization editor lets you iterate on colors and fonts, and the output is a professional-quality vector file you own. TinyTools cannot help here at all — there is no logo generation of any kind.
  • You want an ongoing library of on-brand social media templates. Looka's Brand Kit subscription is its most distinctive feature: every template — LinkedIn header, Instagram post, Facebook cover — is pre-populated with your exact brand colors, logo, and fonts. For a founder who regularly posts on social media and wants everything to look consistent without opening Figma each time, this has genuine value. TinyTools has no templating system.
  • You're launching a physical business that needs print materials. Business cards, letterhead, stickers, and similar print assets are built into the Looka Brand Kit and integrate with print partners. If you're opening a café, studio, or consultancy and need a consistent print presence, Looka handles the full stack — logo through physical collateral — in a way TinyTools can't.
  • You want a cohesive visual identity delivered as a system. Looka's AI pairs logo, color palette, and typeface as a single brand system. The result is intentionally harmonious — colors and fonts that work together. Assembling that level of cohesion manually using separate tools requires more design judgment than most non-designers have. Looka does this work for you.
  • The ~$65 vector package is within your budget. For most business use cases, paying $65 once for a vector logo you own outright is reasonable. It's significantly cheaper than even a junior freelancer, and Looka's output quality has improved substantially in recent years. If your project has any commercial intent, this is a fair investment.

When TinyTools is the better choice

TinyTools wins for situations where you already have a logo, need specific supporting assets, or aren't ready to commit to a paid product yet.

  • You have a logo and need to deploy it on the web. If you have a logo file — from a designer, from a previous brand, or from a tool like Looka — TinyTools handles the next steps for free. The favicon generator converts your PNG into the full browser icon set and web manifest. The color palette generator extracts and documents your exact brand hex values. The background remover cleans up a flat PNG with a white background. All three in under five minutes, no account required.
  • You want to extract exact hex values from a brand reference. Looka's color palettes are generated as part of the logo flow — they're not designed to be queried independently. TinyTools' standalone color palette generator lets you input any seed color or image URL and extract a harmonious set of hex values, which is useful for documenting an existing brand or generating variations from a reference image.
  • You need a favicon set but already have your logo. The favicon generator is one of the more useful standalone tools on TinyTools — it handles the tedious part of favicon deployment (generating the 6–8 required sizes, producing the web manifest JSON, naming files correctly) without requiring you to touch an image editor. Looka includes a logo-to-favicon step in its flow, but it's not accessible standalone and doesn't export the manifest snippet.
  • Privacy is a factor. Background removal and color extraction in TinyTools run in your browser — your image files are not sent to any server. For agency work, client logos, or any file you're not comfortable uploading to a third-party cloud service, this matters. Looka processes everything server-side.
  • You're validating an idea and don't want to spend money yet. If you're at the "does this work?" stage, a paid logo is premature. Use TinyTools to pick a brand color, make a text-initial favicon from the favicon generator, and put up a landing page. Revisit Looka once the idea has traction. The $20–$65 investment makes more sense when you know what you're building.
  • You need a one-off background removal for a logo PNG. Looka doesn't offer background removal. If you received a logo from a designer as a flat JPEG or a PNG with a white background, TinyTools' background remover handles it in-browser for free, producing a transparent PNG ready for dark-mode UIs, overlaid placement, or print compositing.

Verdict for three specific use cases

1. Founder shipping a SaaS product this month

You're building a real product and need a logo, favicon, brand colors, and eventually social presence. Recommendation: Looka for the logo, TinyTools for deployment. Go through Looka's flow, pick a concept, and buy the $65 vector package — it includes SVG, PNG, and the palette reference. Then bring the exported logo PNG into TinyTools' favicon generator to produce the complete browser icon set, and use the color palette generator to export the exact hex values into your CSS variables. The Looka Brand Kit subscription is worth considering once you're posting regularly on social media. The two products are genuinely complementary at this stage.

2. Freelancer processing existing client brand files

Your client has a logo but hasn't set up web assets — no favicon, no documented palette, logo only available as a JPEG with a white background. Recommendation: TinyTools entirely. Run the JPEG through the background remover to get a clean transparent PNG, feed that into the favicon generator for all icon sizes and the web manifest, then use the color palette generator to document the exact brand hex values for the development team. This entire workflow costs nothing, takes under ten minutes, and requires no account sign-up on your client's behalf. Looka is irrelevant here — the logo already exists.

3. Side project at the validation stage

You have an idea, a domain, and maybe a landing page — but you haven't validated that anyone wants it. Recommendation: TinyTools only. Skip the logo entirely for now. Pick a primary brand color with the color palette generator, generate a letter-initial favicon using the favicon generator, and focus your energy on the product and first users. Looka's $20 basic logo is a reasonable purchase once you have evidence that the project is going somewhere — it's premature when you're still figuring out the core problem. Buying a polished brand identity before product-market fit is optimizing in the wrong direction.

The Brand Kit question

Looka's Brand Kit subscription (~$96/year) is the feature that has no equivalent in TinyTools, and it's worth understanding whether you actually need it. The pitch is compelling: every time you need a LinkedIn post or Instagram story, you open a pre-branded template instead of starting from a blank Figma file. In practice, the Brand Kit is most useful for people who post consistently on social media and want a frictionless way to stay on-brand without design work. If you post occasionally and don't mind opening Canva or Figma, the subscription is probably not necessary. If you're running a content-heavy brand or a business where consistent social presence matters, it's a reasonable annual spend — roughly the cost of a single freelance social post.

How Looka compares to LogoAI (a related question)

If you're comparing Looka to LogoAI, the main differences are: Looka leans heavier on the Brand Kit subscription model and the ongoing template library; LogoAI tends toward simpler one-time purchase packages without the subscription layer. The logo generation quality is broadly comparable between the two. Looka is the better choice if you want the ongoing social media templates; LogoAI may be simpler if you just want a logo file. Neither is dramatically better than the other for the core logo-generation use case.

The no-signup angle

Looka's entire experience is gated behind an account — you can see watermarked previews before signing up, but any customization or download requires an account and a purchase. That's a deliberate part of the business model. TinyTools has no account system because there's no business model to protect. For the three tools most relevant to brand asset workflows — color palette, favicon generator, background remover — you can go from nothing to a finished file in under two minutes with zero friction. The same no-signup philosophy applies across the rest of TinyTools: the OG image generator, SEO meta generator, and other utilities follow the same pattern.

Verdict

Looka and TinyTools aren't competing for the same job. Looka answers: "I need a logo and brand identity and I don't have one." TinyTools answers: "I have a logo and I need to deploy it on the web, or I need individual brand utilities for free." They serve sequential steps in the same workflow rather than the same step.

If you're evaluating Looka specifically, the honest assessment is that it does its core job well — the logo generation quality is solid for the price, and the Brand Kit subscription is genuinely useful for social-media-heavy brands. The main reasons to look elsewhere are price sensitivity at the validation stage, or the need for standalone tools (favicon generation, background removal, palette extraction) that Looka doesn't offer. TinyTools fills those gaps; it doesn't replace Looka's core offering.
