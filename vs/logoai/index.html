TinyTools vs LogoAI (2026)

Updated May 23, 2026 · ~6 min read · Honest comparison, not a takedown
The 30-second answer. LogoAI is a paid AI logo generator — you describe your brand, it produces polished vector logo concepts with typography and color palettes, and you buy a package to download the final files. TinyTools doesn't generate logos from scratch. Instead, it gives you the surrounding toolkit for free: a color palette generator for picking brand colors, a favicon generator for turning any image into site icons, and a background remover for cleaning up logo PNGs. If you need a logo designed for you by AI, LogoAI is a legitimate option. If you have a logo and need the supporting assets — or you're working within Figma or with a designer — TinyTools fills the gaps for free.

What each one actually is

LogoAI (logoai.com) is a fully managed AI logo design service. You answer a few questions about your company name, industry, and preferred style, and the AI generates a set of logo concepts — wordmarks, icon-plus-text combinations, monograms — each with a suggested color palette and typeface pairing. Once you find a concept you like, you customize it in a browser editor (swap colors, adjust spacing, try alternate font weights) and then purchase a download package to get the final SVG, PNG, and PDF files. LogoAI also offers brand kit add-ons: social media cover templates, business card mockups, and letterhead. There is no free download — the preview is watermarked, and the end-to-end experience is gated behind a one-time payment.
TinyTools is a collection of free, no-signup utilities that cover individual steps in the brand-building workflow. The three most relevant here: the color palette generator extracts a harmonious palette from any seed color or image URL, outputting hex values ready to paste into Figma or a CSS file. The favicon generator takes any PNG or SVG and exports the full set of icon sizes (16px, 32px, 180px, 192px, 512px) plus a web manifest snippet. The background remover strips the background from any product or logo PNG entirely in-browser, no upload to a server. None of these replace a logo; they complete the workflow around a logo you already have.

When LogoAI is the better choice

Being honest about this: LogoAI is solving a problem TinyTools doesn't try to solve. If you need any of the following, LogoAI wins cleanly.

  • You need a logo and don't have a designer. This is the core use case. You're launching a product, you don't have a Figma file with a logo, and you need something professional enough to put on a landing page and business card. LogoAI will generate polished vector concepts in minutes. TinyTools can't help you here — it has no logo generation whatsoever.
  • You want a full brand kit, not just a logo file. LogoAI's higher-tier packages include social media cover images, letterhead, and business card templates sized for common print specs. If you're launching a brand and need those assets delivered in one purchase, that's what LogoAI is designed for.
  • You want a browser editor to tweak colors, fonts, and spacing. LogoAI's in-browser customizer lets you swap typefaces, adjust kerning, try different color palettes, and preview alternate icon layouts before committing. There's genuine design flexibility built into the product — it's not just "here's a flat image."
  • The $29–$99 price point is acceptable. For most commercial use cases, a one-time logo purchase in this range is reasonable. If you're launching something you care about and you want a clean vector file you legally own, paying for it makes sense and LogoAI is a fair value compared to hiring a freelance designer.
  • You want something that feels cohesive out of the box. LogoAI generates the logo, palette, and typography together as a system. The result is a brand identity that hangs together visually from the start, rather than something you assemble from separate tools.

When TinyTools is the better choice

There's a meaningful subset of use cases where TinyTools is the right answer — and they tend to be situations where you already have a logo or don't need one yet.

  • You already have a logo and need the surrounding assets. You have a logo file from a designer, a previous brand, or a tool like LogoAI — and now you need a favicon set, a brand color reference, or a clean PNG with the background removed. TinyTools handles all three for free, in your browser, in under a minute each.
  • You want to extract a palette from an existing brand image. The color palette generator takes an image URL or seed hex value and produces a harmonious 5–8 color palette with hex codes. Useful for documenting an existing brand identity, matching colors from a reference image, or generating variations you can take into a design tool.
  • You need favicons for a side project right now. The favicon generator takes any PNG — including a logo you already have — and spits out all the sizes a browser expects, plus a web manifest JSON snippet you can paste directly into your ``. No account, no email, done in 30 seconds.
  • You have a logo PNG with a white background that needs to be transparent. The background remover handles this in-browser, no upload to a third-party server. Useful when you receive a logo from a designer as a flat PNG and need the transparent version for dark-mode UI or overlaid placement.
  • You're testing a product idea and don't want to spend $29 yet. Validation stage is real. If you're still figuring out whether your project has legs, buying a logo is premature. Use a placeholder, extract a color palette, generate a simple text-based favicon from the favicon generator, and revisit the logo purchase once you have users.
  • Privacy matters. Background removal and color extraction in TinyTools happen locally in your browser — your logo files and images are not uploaded to TinyTools' servers (there are no servers). For client work or confidential brand work, that matters.

Verdict for three specific use cases

1. Founder launching a SaaS product this week

You're shipping a landing page on Friday and need a logo, favicon, and consistent brand colors. Recommendation: LogoAI first, then TinyTools. Use LogoAI to generate a logo concept and buy the basic package (~$29). Then bring the exported PNG into TinyTools' favicon generator to produce the full icon set, and use the color palette generator to extract and document the exact hex values. The two tools are complementary — LogoAI creates the asset, TinyTools packages it for the web.

2. Freelancer cleaning up client brand assets

A client sends you a folder with a logo in JPEG format with a white background, no favicon, and no documented color palette. Recommendation: TinyTools. Run the logo through the background remover, generate favicons from the clean PNG using the favicon generator, and use the color palette generator to extract and document the brand colors. The entire cleanup workflow costs zero dollars and takes under five minutes — no LogoAI needed when the logo already exists.

3. Indie hacker validating a side project

You have an idea and want to put up a landing page without committing real money. Recommendation: TinyTools. Pick a brand color with the color palette generator, make a simple letter-icon favicon in the favicon generator, and skip the logo entirely until you have paying users. LogoAI is a fair investment once a project has proven traction — it's premature at the "does this idea work?" stage.

The design quality question

It's worth naming the elephant in the room: AI-generated logos from tools like LogoAI, Looka, and similar services have a recognizable aesthetic. The typeface-plus-icon combinations look clean, but experienced designers and founders often recognize them as AI-generated. That's not necessarily a problem — for many early-stage products, "looks professional" is the right bar, not "looks bespoke." But if you're building a brand you plan to invest in long-term, a logo from a human designer (even a junior one via a marketplace like 99designs) will produce something more distinctive than an AI generator at any price point. LogoAI is the right tool for "good enough fast," not for "this brand should be around in ten years."

The no-signup angle

LogoAI requires an account before you can save or customize any logo concept, and you need to purchase before you can download an unwatermarked file. That friction is intentional — the entire business model depends on converting preview sessions into purchases. TinyTools has no business model to protect, so there's no gate. For users who are allergic to the "sign up to see the result" pattern, TinyTools' tools are structured specifically to avoid it. You might appreciate the same philosophy in other parts of the stack — our OG image generator and SEO meta generator also operate entirely without accounts.

Verdict

LogoAI and TinyTools don't compete head-to-head — they cover different parts of the brand-building workflow. LogoAI generates logos; TinyTools processes the assets you already have. The honest answer to "which should I use?" is almost always "depends where you are in the process." No logo yet and need one? LogoAI (or a designer). Logo exists and need the web-ready assets? TinyTools.

If you're shopping for a free alternative to LogoAI's logo generation specifically — there isn't a great one. The honest free options are either much lower quality (clipart-style SVG generators) or require more design skill than most people want to invest (Figma from scratch). If price is the blocker, LogoAI's basic package at ~$29 one-time is genuinely reasonable for commercial use, and you should evaluate it on that basis rather than searching for a free substitute that doesn't exist yet.
