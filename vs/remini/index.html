TinyTools vs Remini (2026)

Updated May 23, 2026 · ~6 min read · Honest comparison, not a takedown
The 30-second answer. Remini is a consumer AI photo enhancer built for restoring and sharpening old, blurry, or low-resolution photos — it runs on your phone, has a subscription, and uses server-side AI models to produce genuinely impressive results on faces and fine detail. TinyTools' AI image upscaler is a free, browser-only tool that runs a local ONNX super-resolution model — no upload, no account, no credit system, unlimited images. If you need to restore degraded family photos or selfies with portrait AI, Remini is the category leader. If you need to upscale screenshots, product images, or illustrations without sending files to a server, TinyTools does it instantly for free.

What each one actually is

Remini launched in 2019 as an iOS app and quickly went viral for its ability to make blurry, pixelated, or damaged old photographs look sharp and detailed again. Its core technology is a face-focused generative AI model that predicts and reconstructs facial features even from a few pixels of source data — the results on portraits are often genuinely startling. Over time Remini added HD video enhancement, AI avatar generation, AI baby photos (predict what a child might look like), age filters, and style-transfer effects. It's a consumer entertainment and restoration app as much as it is a utility. It has been downloaded hundreds of millions of times across iOS and Android, with a web interface added more recently. All processing happens on Remini's servers; you upload, they process, you download.
TinyTools AI Image Upscaler is a static web page that loads a Real-ESRGAN ONNX model into your browser and runs it using WebAssembly and WebGL acceleration on your local CPU or GPU. The model handles 2× and 4× upscaling for photos, illustrations, and screenshots without any file leaving your device. There are no accounts, no credit bundles, no daily limits, no watermarks, and no API calls made during enhancement. The scope is intentionally narrow: you drop an image, pick a scale factor, download the result. It does not attempt face hallucination, video enhancement, avatar generation, or entertainment effects.

When Remini is the better choice

Pretending a free, browser-only upscaler replaces Remini's portrait AI would be misleading. Choose Remini when:

  • You want to restore old family photos. This is where Remini genuinely shines. Blurry scans of 1970s prints, damaged film photos, passport-era snapshots with a smear of compression — Remini's face reconstruction model can recover detail that simply isn't in the pixel data. A standard upscaler like TinyTools can sharpen edges but cannot hallucinate the missing facial features the way Remini does.
  • The subjects are faces and portraits. Remini's model is heavily tuned for human faces — skin texture, eye sharpness, hair strand separation. If your primary use case is portrait photography rather than product shots, screenshots, or illustrations, it's measurably better at what it does.
  • You want to enhance video. TinyTools has no video pipeline at all. Remini Pro can apply frame-by-frame enhancement to short clips — a capability in a completely different class from anything a browser tool currently offers.
  • You want AI avatar sets or entertainment effects. Remini generates AI avatar packs, predicted baby photos, and age-progression images. These are entertainment features with no equivalent in TinyTools.
  • You're on mobile without access to a desktop browser. Remini's primary experience is a mobile app, well-optimized for iOS and Android. TinyTools' web upscaler works on mobile browsers but performance depends heavily on the device's WebGL support and available RAM — on older phones it can be slow.
  • Processing speed matters more than privacy. Remini's server-side GPU inference is typically faster than in-browser WebAssembly execution on an average laptop or phone, especially for large files.

When TinyTools is the better choice

The honest case for TinyTools is narrower but real:

  • You need to upscale images you can't share with a third party. Internal product mockups, unreleased designs, confidential client imagery, anything under NDA. TinyTools never sees the file — there is no upload, no server log, nothing to audit or breach. For Remini to work, your image has to leave your device.
  • The content isn't a portrait. Remini's strength is faces. For screenshots, UI mockups, illustrations, logos, maps, or product shots without people, TinyTools' Real-ESRGAN model handles non-portrait content more generically — and doesn't try to hallucinate facial features into graphic elements where they don't belong.
  • You need unlimited upscaling right now, for free. Remini's free tier is deliberately limited to push users toward Pro. If you have 50 images to resize for a launch tomorrow and no budget for a subscription, TinyTools is the faster path.
  • You don't want another app or account. Remini is primarily a mobile app that requires sign-in. TinyTools is a URL you paste into any browser — no download, no install, no account, no push notification permission request.
  • You want to work offline. Once the TinyTools page is loaded and the model is cached, you can disconnect from the internet and keep upscaling. Remini's web interface requires connectivity for every image. This matters on planes, in co-working spaces with spotty wifi, or in regions with bandwidth limits.
  • You're already in a TinyTools workflow. If you're using our background remover to cut out product images and our OG image generator to build social preview cards, the upscaler sits naturally in the same tab. No new account, no cross-device friction.

The face hallucination question

It's worth addressing directly because it matters both for use-case fit and for editorial honesty: Remini's face restoration technology does not recover existing detail — it generates plausible detail that the model believes should be there. For old personal photos, that's usually fine and often wonderful. For news photography, forensics, legal documents, or any context where authenticity matters, an AI-hallucinated face is a fabrication, not a restoration. TinyTools' upscaler interpolates and sharpens the pixels that already exist in the image without inventing new ones. That's a meaningful distinction depending on your use case.

Three concrete use cases

1. Parent digitizing 30-year-old family photos from a shoebox

Recommendation: Remini. Old family photos with compression, grain, fading, and the inevitable out-of-focus toddler face are exactly the brief Remini was built for. The face reconstruction model regularly produces results that make people emotional. TinyTools would sharpen what's already there, but Remini can reconstruct a face from a grain cluster. For sentimental restoration, Remini is the correct tool.

2. Indie developer upscaling UI screenshots for an App Store listing

Recommendation: TinyTools. App Store screenshots are UI content — interface elements, typography, icons. They are not portraits. Remini's portrait model is the wrong fit, and uploading pre-launch screenshots to a consumer app's server introduces unnecessary IP risk. TinyTools runs locally, handles graphic content well, and costs nothing. The developer keeps their screenshots private until launch day.

3. Content creator producing vintage-style social media portraits

Recommendation: Remini, with caveats. Remini's aesthetic filters and portrait enhancers are directly useful here, and the mobile app fits the creator workflow of shoot-edit-post. Be aware that the "enhancement" modifies faces algorithmically — the result is stylized, not documentary. That's fine for creative content and worth knowing before applying it to journalism or any context implying photographic authenticity.

Privacy and data handling

This is a meaningful differentiator for some users. Remini is a fully server-side product: every image you enhance is uploaded to their infrastructure, processed on their GPUs, and returned to you. What they retain, for how long, and whether images or derived embeddings contribute to future model training is governed by their privacy policy and changes to it over time. For consumer personal photos this is typically uncontroversial. For professional or confidential imagery — unreleased products, legal evidence, proprietary designs, client work under NDA — it's a real consideration.

TinyTools has no server-side image pipeline. The Real-ESRGAN model is downloaded once and cached in your browser; from that point on, every enhancement runs locally. There is no log entry, no retention period, no opt-out toggle needed. The tradeoff is that you get browser-speed inference rather than GPU-farm inference, and you don't get the portrait-reconstruction capabilities that require Remini's large generative model to have seen your face.

Verdict

Remini is the right tool when the job is portrait restoration or face enhancement — particularly for old, degraded, or low-resolution photos where the goal is making a face look recognizably sharp again. Its mobile-first experience, the entertainment features, and the sheer volume of training that went into the face model make it a genuinely impressive product in its niche. If you're restoring family history or need AI avatar packs, there's no browser-side equivalent that competes honestly.

TinyTools isn't trying to be a portrait studio. It's trying to answer a different question: "I need this image larger and sharper, right now, without sending it anywhere." For non-portrait content, for privacy-sensitive files, for developers and designers who want unlimited free upscaling in a browser tab, and for anyone who resents signing up for yet another app to do one thing — TinyTools is the faster, freer, and more private path. Most people use both tools for different jobs, and that's probably the right answer.
